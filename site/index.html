Shift scheduling for 5 ice-cream stores and 46 staff

Amorino Escalas replaces a weekly PDF rota with one app. Staff send their availability from their phone. The supervisor builds and checks the rota, then publishes it. Everyone sees their shifts, swaps them and gets them in their calendar.

What it does

It covers the whole weekly cycle: collect availability, build the rota, check it, publish it, then handle changes after publication.

Availability

A mobile-first weekly form for staff. The supervisor sees who has answered and who still needs a reminder.

Schedule editor

A per-store weekly grid with drag-and-drop or tap-to-assign, search, contract filters and live planned hours for each contract.

Suggestions

Ranks candidates for an empty slot by availability, contract hours, rest days and a recency-weighted affinity from past schedules.

Conflict checks

Flags hours over or under contract, shifts on unavailable days, two shifts on one day, and junior staff opening or closing alone.

Versioned publication

Edits stay in a draft. Publishing is explicit, staff always see the last published version, and later changes are recorded.

Swaps & donations

Staff request a swap or offer a shift. A supervisor approves it, and the rota updates atomically.

Management overview

Coverage against target, planned hours, response rate and pending decisions per week, with a presentation mode.

Exports

PDFs per week, store or person (ReportLab), an hours CSV, and a personal .ics calendar feed for each employee.

Operations

Online backups, verified restore, a pre-flight config check, and rehearsed migration and recovery, with systemd and nginx examples.

Screens

Captured from the app's built-in demo mode. All people are fictional. The interface is in European Portuguese, the language of the team that uses it.

Engineering

It holds real staff data, so security and correctness were requirements from the first version.

Authentication

PINs are hashed with PBKDF2-SHA256 and checked in constant time, with a dummy hash for unknown users so timing reveals nothing. Failed logins are rate-limited. New accounts are activated with single-use 256-bit invites, of which only the SHA-256 is stored.

Sessions & requests

Session IDs rotate at login and expire on the server. Cookies are HttpOnly and SameSite. Every state-changing request needs a CSRF token, and every response carries strict security headers. A reset revokes the old PIN, sessions and calendar token at once.

XSS discipline

Jinja autoescaping is used with no |safe overrides. DOM built from API data uses textContent or an escaping helper, because notes typed by staff are rendered in the supervisor's browser.

Concurrency

One worker process with threads, SQLite in WAL mode, and conditional atomic updates for publication, invites and swap approval. Tests cover concurrent publication.

Time handling

Handles overnight shifts such as 16:00-01:30 (+1), lunch-break rules, Lisbon dates on hosts without tzdata, year boundaries and Portuguese public holidays.

Testing & operations

502 pytest tests cover routes, authorization, rules, exports and recovery, and Node runs the browser-side functions in the same suite. The app also has backup, restore, verification and pre-flight scripts, plus a timed recovery rehearsal.

My role

I designed and built the whole thing: data model, backend, interface, tests and operations tooling. I built it for the team I work with at the Amorino stores in Porto and Gaia.

  • Turned the existing weekly PDF rota and the supervisor's informal rules into a data model and checks: contract hours, ranks, rest days and opening and closing cover.
  • Designed a mobile-first interface in European Portuguese for staff, and a dense planning view for the supervisor.
  • Wrote an import tool that parses past schedule PDFs, so suggestions learn from real history.
  • Documented the security posture and the deployment, backup and recovery procedures.
